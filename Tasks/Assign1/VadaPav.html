Dough ingredients
2 cups all-purpose flour
1 tsp carom seeds (ajwain)
¾ tsp salt
¼ cup ghee or oil
6 to 8 tbsp water 
Filling ingredients
4 medium potatoes, boiled and crumbled
½ cup green peas, boiled or frozen
1 tbsp oil
¾ tsp cumin seeds
1 tbsp finely chopped ginger
1–2 green chilies, finely chopped
1 tsp ground coriander
½ tsp red chili powder
½ tsp garam masala
½ tsp amchur (dry mango powder), for a tangy flavor
Salt to taste
2 tbsp chopped cilantro
Oil for deep-frying 
Instructions
1. Make the dough
In a large bowl, combine the all-purpose flour, carom seeds, and salt.
Add the ghee or oil and rub it into the flour with your fingertips for about 3–4 minutes, until the mixture resembles coarse breadcrumbs. A handful should hold its shape when pressed together.
Add water gradually, kneading just enough to form a stiff, firm dough. Do not overwork it.
Cover the dough with a damp cloth and let it rest for at least 30–40 minutes. 
2. Prepare the filling
Heat 1 tbsp of oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them sizzle for a few seconds.
Add the chopped ginger and green chilies, and sauté for about a minute until fragrant.
Add the crumbled potatoes and green peas to the pan. Mix well.
Stir in the coriander powder, red chili powder, garam masala, amchur, and salt.
Mix everything together and sauté for 2–3 minutes, until the spices are well combined.
Remove the filling from the heat and stir in the chopped cilantro. Let the mixture cool completely before using. 
3. Assemble the samosas
Give the rested dough a quick knead and divide it into 6 equal portions.
Roll one dough ball into an oval shape about 6–7 inches in diameter.
Cut the oval in half to form two semicircles.
Take one semicircle and apply water along the straight edge. Fold the two ends of the straight edge together to form a cone, pressing the seam firmly to seal it.
Spoon 1–2 tablespoons of the potato filling into the cone. Do not overfill.
Apply water to the open edges and pinch them together to seal the top. You can make a small pleat on one side to help the samosa stand upright.
Repeat with the remaining dough and filling, keeping the finished samosas covered with a damp cloth to prevent them from drying out. 
4. Fry the samosas
Heat enough oil for deep-frying in a deep pan or kadai. The oil should be moderately hot, not sizzling. To test the temperature, drop a small piece of dough into the oil—it should rise slowly, not immediately.
Gently place the samosas into the oil in batches, being careful not to overcrowd the pan.
Fry on a low to medium-low flame for about 10–12 minutes, or until the pastry is cooked through and firm.
Increase the heat to medium and continue to fry, flipping occasionally, until the samosas are golden brown and crispy.
Remove the fried samosas and drain them on a wire rack or paper towels.
Allow the oil temperature to drop slightly before frying the next batch.
Serve hot with your favorite chutney.
