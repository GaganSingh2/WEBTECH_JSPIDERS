Ingredients
Idli rice (parboiled rice): 2 cups
Urad dal (split or whole husked black gram): ½ cup
Thick poha (flattened rice): ¼ cup (optional, for extra softness)
Fenugreek seeds: ¼ teaspoon (optional, for flavor and fermentation)
Non-iodized salt (rock or sea salt): 1 teaspoon
Oil: for greasing the molds 
Instructions
Soaking (4–6 hours)
Prepare the rice and dal. In separate bowls, thoroughly rinse the idli rice and urad dal with fresh water until the water runs clear.
Add poha and fenugreek. Add the washed poha to the bowl with the rice and the fenugreek seeds to the bowl with the urad dal.
Soak. Cover both bowls with water and let them soak for 4 to 6 hours. 
Grinding and fermenting (8–12 hours)
Grind the dal. Drain the water from the urad dal. Using a blender or wet grinder, grind the dal and fenugreek seeds with a little cold water to create a smooth, light, and fluffy paste. Using cold water prevents the batter from heating up during grinding.
Grind the rice. Drain the water from the rice and poha. Grind them with fresh water until the rice has a slightly coarse, semolina-like texture. Do not grind it to a very fine paste.
Mix the batter. Combine the urad dal and rice batters in a large, non-airtight bowl. Use your hands to mix them well for a few minutes, as the warmth of your hands can assist with fermentation. Add the non-iodized salt and stir gently to combine.
Ferment. Loosely cover the bowl and place it in a warm spot for 8 to 12 hours, or until the batter has doubled in volume and is light, airy, and has a mildly sour aroma. Fermentation times may be longer in colder climates. 
Steaming the idlis (10–15 minutes)
Prepare the steamer. Fill an idli steamer, pressure cooker, or instant pot with 2 to 2.5 cups of water and bring it to a boil over medium-high heat.
Grease the molds. Lightly grease the idli molds with oil.
Pour the batter. Stir the fermented batter gently without overmixing. Pour the batter into the greased molds, filling them about three-quarters full.
Steam. Place the idli stand in the steamer. Steam for 10 to 15 minutes. If using a pressure cooker, remove the vent weight/whistle.
Check for doneness. Insert a toothpick or knife. If it comes out clean, the idlis are done. Avoid overcooking, as this can make the idlis hard.
Unmold and serve. Let the idlis rest in the molds for 2–3 minutes. Then, use a spoon or knife dipped in water to gently loosen and remove them. Serve hot with sambar and coconut chutney.
